# Behaviors

<Art name="penguin" size={95} />

A **behavior** is ready-made movement or logic you add to an object in one tap: walk and jump with the joystick, drive like a car, follow the player, spin, float up and down… Add one with **Inspect → ＋ Add behavior**.

<Row>
  <Phone name="add-behavior-menu" caption="＋ Add behavior" />
  <Phone name="add-behavior-menu-end" caption="…more" />
</Row>

Each behavior card has a switch to turn it **on/off** and 🗑 to remove it. Events can do the same while playing (**Enable/disable behavior**) and change any setting (**Set behavior setting**); scripts use `entity.setBehaviorParam('platformer', 'speed', 8)`. In expressions: `Player.behavior("platformer", "speed")`.

## 🏃 Platformer {/* #platformer */}

<Side shot="behavior-platformer" crop>

Walk with the joystick (or WASD / arrows) and jump with a button. Needs a **dynamic Physics body** — adding the behavior adds one that doesn’t tip over, and a Collider.

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Speed** | 5 | Walking speed (m/s). |
| **Jump strength** | 7 | How high it jumps. |
| **Jump button** | jump | The touch button that jumps (see *Touch controls*). |
| **Turn to direction** | on | Face the way it moves. |
| **Camera relative** | on | “Up” on the joystick means *away from the camera*, not a fixed direction. |
| **Animate** | on | Play the model’s idle / walk / sprint / jump / fall clips automatically. |

</Side>

## 🕹️ Top-down movement {/* #topdown */}

<Side shot="behavior-topdown" crop>

Move in 8 directions on the ground with the joystick — no jumping. For top-down, twin-stick and rolling-ball games. Works with or without a Physics body (with one, it pushes and gets blocked by things).

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Speed** | 5 | Movement speed. |
| **Turn to direction** | on | Face the way it moves. |
| **Camera relative** | on | Joystick directions follow the camera. |
| **Animate** | on | Idle / walk / sprint clips automatically. |

</Side>

## 🏎️ Car {/* #car */}

<Side shot="behavior-car" crop>

Drive with the joystick: **up = gas**, **down = brake, then reverse**, **left/right = steer**. Real ray-cast vehicle physics with suspension. Needs a **dynamic Physics body**.

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Speed** | 15 | Top speed (m/s). |
| **Acceleration** | 10 | How fast it speeds up. |
| **Steering** | 30 | Largest steering angle (degrees). |
| **Grip** | 1 | Tyre grip. Lower = drifty, higher = sticky. |
| **Brake** | 1 | Braking strength. |
| **Wheels** | — | Which nodes of the car model are the wheels (they spin and steer). Tap to choose; mark the front ones. |
| **Driver** | player | Who drives: **player** (joystick, WASD or arrow keys), **ai** (drives itself along the Path) or **none** (parked: no gas, no steering). Change it from events with *Set behavior setting*, e.g. to start a race after a countdown. |
| **Path** | — | Waypoint objects that form a closed racing line, e.g. `#waypoint`. Needed for **ai**. They are visited in the order of their `index` variable, or by name (“Waypoint 2” before “Waypoint 10”). |

Wheels are found automatically in models whose parts are named “wheel…” (like the Kenney car kit). Otherwise pick them in **Wheels**; without wheels it drives on 4 invisible wheels at the corners.

With a **Path**, every car keeps two instance variables up to date: `progress` (laps driven, e.g. `1.4`) and `lap` (the lap it is on, from 1). Compare cars' `progress` to work out race positions. See the [Street Racer showcase](./showcase/street-racer).

</Side>

## ⛵ Boat {/* #boat */}

Sail on [Water](./world/water) with the joystick: **up = forward**, **down = reverse** (slower), **left/right = steer**. The boat floats high on the waves, speeds up and turns smoothly, hardly drifts sideways, leans into turns and rocks gently. On land it has no power. Needs a **dynamic Physics body** (a **Collider** on *Auto* fits a boat model).

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Speed** | 8 | Top speed (m/s). Reverse is slower. |
| **Turn speed** | 70 | How fast it turns (degrees per second) once it is moving; it turns less when nearly still, like a real boat. |
| **Acceleration** | 3 | How quickly it speeds up. With the stick released it glides to a stop. |
| **Driver** | player | **player** (joystick, WASD or arrow keys) or **none** (nobody at the wheel). |

The boat keeps its speed in the instance variable `speed` (m/s). Put **Camera follow** on the camera with **Target** = the boat and **Stay behind** on.

:::tip Getting in and out
Give the boat **Driver = none** at the start. When the player touches the boat and presses a button: *Attach to* the boat (the player rides along), set the player's Platformer off and the boat's **Driver** to **player** with *Set behavior setting*, and point the camera at the boat. Do the reverse to get off.
:::

## 🎥 Camera follow {/* #camera-follow */}

<Side shot="behavior-cameraFollow" crop>

Put it **on the camera**: it smoothly follows a target.

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Target** | Player | The object to follow. |
| **Offset X / Y / Z** | 0, 6, 10 | Where the camera stays, relative to the target (6 up, 10 behind). |
| **Smoothing** | 5 | Higher = snappier, lower = floatier. |
| **Look at target** | on | Keep pointing at the target. |
| **Stay behind** | off | Turn the offset with the target’s heading — a chase camera for cars and runners. |
| **Avoid walls** | off | When a wall, building or other **static** body is between the target and the camera, the camera comes in front of it instead of showing the inside of the wall. Moving objects (other cars, enemies) don’t count. The wall needs a Collider that covers its shape, e.g. *Convex* for models. |
| **Ignore tag** | (empty) | With Avoid walls on: objects with this tag never push the camera in, e.g. `#tree` in a forest, so it doesn't jump forward every time a trunk passes in front. They still block the player. |
| **Mouse look** | off | Let the player turn the camera around the target: on a computer, click the game and move the mouse (Esc frees the mouse); with a gamepad, the right stick; on a phone, drag the free side of the screen. Platformer and Top-down movement follow the camera, so *forward* is always away from it. With **Stay behind** on, the camera drifts back behind the target a second after the player stops looking. |
| **Look speed** | 1 | How fast Mouse look turns. 2 = twice as fast. |

</Side>

## ➤ Bullet {/* #bullet */}

<Side shot="behavior-bullet" crop>

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |

</Side>

## 〰️ Wave (sine) {/* #sine */}

<Side shot="behavior-sine" crop>

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Property** | y | What waves: `x`, `y`, `z`, `angle`, `scale` or `opacity`. |

## 🎯 Follow target {/* #follow */}

<Side shot="behavior-follow" crop>

Moves toward another object — chasing enemies, pets, homing missiles.

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Target** | Player | What to follow. |
| **Speed** | 2 | Meters per second. |
| **Stop distance** | 1 | Stops this close. |
| **Look at** | on | Face the target. |
| **Animate** | on | Walk / idle clips automatically. |
| **Pathfinding** | off | Walk around walls, rocks and buildings instead of pushing into them. It finds a way round anything **static** (a Physics body set to *Static*, with a Collider), re-plans as the target moves, and stands at the closest spot when the target can't be reached. Other moving objects don't count. Works on flat ground; it doesn't plan jumps or stairs between floors. |

</Side>

### Walk to a point {/* #move-to-position */}

To send something to a **spot** instead of after an object, use the **Move to position** action (*Object* group): it walks there over the next frames, around walls when **Go around walls** is on, and **On arrived** runs when it gets there (or as close as it can). **Stop moving** cancels it, and *Is moving to a position* is true on the way. It works with or without a Physics body; a movement behavior on the same object waits while it walks, and the joystick takes over again at once.

Tap to move — two rows, with the spot under the finger from **`touchWorldX`** / **`touchWorldZ`**:

```text
On screen tapped   →  Move Player to (touchWorldX, touchWorldZ) at speed 4
On Player arrived  →  Play sound "Click"
```

A patrol: **On Guard arrived** → **Move Guard to** the next point. In scripts: `entity.moveTo(x, z, speed)`, `entity.stopMoving()`, and `game.touchWorld()` for the tapped spot (the **Tap to move** snippet).

## 🗑️ Destroy when far {/* #destroy-offscreen */}

<Side shot="behavior-destroyOffscreen" crop>

Removes the object when it falls or goes too far — keeps games with many bullets or spawned obstacles fast.

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Kill Y** | −20 | Destroyed when it falls below this height. |
| **Max distance** | 200 | Destroyed this far from the camera (0 = never). |

</Side>

## ✋ Draggable {/* #draggable */}

| Setting | Default | What it does |

## ❤️ Health {/* #health */}

<Side shot="behavior-health" crop>

Gives the object health: it loses some with the **Damage** action, gets it back with **Heal**, and dies at 0. Use it for the player's lives, enemies that take several hits, crates that break.

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Max health** | 3 | Health at the start, and the most **Heal** gives back. |
| **Invincible time** | 0.5 | Seconds after a hit when more damage does nothing, so one touch doesn't cost several lives. 0 = every hit counts. |
| **Flash** | on | Blink while invincible. |
| **Knockback** | 0 | Push it away from what hit it (the *From* object of **Damage**), in m/s. Needs a dynamic Physics body. |
| **Show bar** | off | A small health bar floats above it once it is hurt: green, then orange, then red. |
| **Destroy on death** | on | Remove it at 0 health, right after its **On death** event. Turn off for the player, to show a game over screen instead. |

</Side>

Health is kept in the object's own variable **`health`**, so you can show it (`Player.health` in a progress bar) or compare it like any variable. Setting `health` above 0 again brings a dead object back, e.g. on respawn.

In the event sheet, **Health** has:

- **Damage** *amount* (from *object*) and **Heal** *amount* — actions.
- **On hurt** and **On death** — conditions that run when it happens. The object is still there during *On death*, so you can play an explosion at it and add its points.

```text
On Bullet collides with #enemy  →  Damage #enemy by 1 (from Bullet), Destroy Bullet
On #enemy death                 →  Spawn particles "explosion" at #enemy, add #enemy.points to Score
```

Damage first, then destroy the bullet: a destroyed object can't be the *From* of the knockback. In scripts: `entity.damage(1, bullet)`, `entity.heal(1)`.
