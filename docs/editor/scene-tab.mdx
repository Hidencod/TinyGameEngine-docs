# Scene tab

<Art name="tree-small" size={85} />

The **Scene** tab holds settings for the whole scene you are editing, plus the project’s variables and touch controls.

<Side shot="dock-scene">

At the top: the scene you are editing (tap to switch — see [Scenes](./scenes)), a **★ Start scene** badge if the game starts here, and **⚙ Project** to open [Project settings](./project-settings).

The cards below can be folded by tapping their title.

</Side>

<Crop name="scene-tab-full" caption="The whole Scene tab (Roll-a-ball template)" width={340} />

## Scene

**Name** — the scene’s name, used by the *Go to scene* action.

## Environment

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Background** | **Color** (a plain color), **Sky image (360°)** or **Sky (sun and clouds)**. |
| **Color** | The background color. |
| **Ambient light** | Color of the soft light that reaches everything, even in shadow. |
| **Ambient strength** | How strong it is (0–2). Lower it for a darker, moodier scene. |
| **Fog** | Fades far things into the fog color. Also hides the edge of big worlds and helps performance. |
| **Fog type** | *Linear*: clear until **Fog starts at**, solid at **Fog fully at**. *Thick (exponential)*: gets thicker with distance, set by **Fog thickness** (about 0.01 is a light haze, 0.1 is dense). *Ground (height)*: like thick, but densest near the ground and thinner higher up, for morning mist in a valley (below). |
| **Fog color / starts at / fully at** | Color, the distance where fog begins, and the distance where it is solid (Linear only). |
| **Tone mapping** | Squeezes bright light into a film-like picture: *None*, *ACES (film)*, *Neutral* or *AgX*. Pick one and an **Exposure** slider appears (lower = darker). |
| **Glow (bloom)** | Bright things glow. See [Glow](#glow). |

### Sky image

<Side shot="sky-picker">

Choose **Background → Sky image (360°)** (or tap the sky image) and pick one of:

- **Free skies…** — day, morning, night, space, alien (by Kenney, free).
- **Import a 360° image…** — a 2:1 panorama (equirectangular) PNG, JPG or WebP, or a high-range **.hdr** or **.exr** panorama (from Poly Haven, say). An HDR sky has real bright sun and sky values, so it lights the scene and reflects in shiny materials much better than a plain picture; use **Tone mapping** (below) so the bright parts don't clip. The Files tab shows a toned-down preview of it.
- One of your project’s images.

</Side>

Then:

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Sky image** | Change the image. |
| **Turn sky** | Rotate the panorama (0–360°) to put the sun where you want it. |
| **Sky brightness** | 1 = the image as it is. |
| **Light with sky** | Objects with the **Realistic** material get reflections and soft light from the sky. |
| **Loading color** | Shown until the sky has loaded. |

### Sky (sun and clouds)

A sky drawn live, with a sun and clouds, and it lights the scene. No image needed.

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Look** | Ready-made skies: *Day*, *Clear noon*, *Sunset*, *Ocean dusk* (a low sun over the water) and *Overcast*. It sets the sun, haze and cloud sliders below; change any of them and the Look reads *Custom*. |
| **Sun height / Sun direction** | Where the sun is: degrees above the horizon (0–90) and around you. Low gives a sunset. The sky does not follow your **Sun** light object: turn that light to match (a new scene's Sun points at height 41, direction 42). |
| **Sun size** | The size of the sun's disc. |
| **Haze** | More haze whitens the sky and warms the horizon. |
| **Sky blue** | How blue the clear sky is. |
| **Clouds / Cloud thickness / Cloud height** | How much of the sky is covered, how dense the clouds are, and how high they drift. |
| **Light with sky** | The sky lights objects with the **Realistic** material (reflections and soft light). **Sky light** sets how strongly. |
| **Sun light / Sun light strength** | Adds a sun light (no shadows) from where the sky's sun is: it warms toward sunset and dims as the sun sets. Off by default, because the scene already has its own **Sun** light. |

Choosing it the first time also turns on **Tone mapping: ACES**, which a bright sky needs to look right.

### Glow

**Glow (bloom)** makes anything brighter than a level glow softly: lamps, fire, neon, the sun. **Glow strength**, **Glow spread** and **Glow above** (how bright something must be before it glows) tune it. It is drawn in the game, not in the editor view, and costs speed on weak phones, so keep it off unless you want it. Materials with a bright **Emissive** color are what glow.

### Ground fog

With **Fog type: Ground (height)**, the fog lies low like mist. **Fog level** is the height (world Y, in meters) where it has its **Fog thickness**; above that it thins out, faster the higher **Fog thinning** is (0.15 clears about every 10 m of height, 1 is a thin layer). Below the level it gets thicker still. Looking down through it is thicker than looking along it, so hills poke out above it. Put the level a little above your ground to make a valley fill with mist.

It works on everything lit, the water and particles, in the game and in the editor view. It doesn't tint the sky color (the sky is not fogged), so match the **Fog color** to the horizon.

### Screen effects

Effects drawn over the finished picture, in the game only (not in the editor view). They are all off until you change them, and a player can switch them all off with **Graphics quality** (Low does).

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Edge smoothing (FXAA)** | Smooths jagged edges cheaply. Use it instead of **Smooth edges (antialias)** in Project settings, which is slow when a scene also has glow or other effects. |
| **Vignette** | Darker corners. |
| **Color fringe** | Red and blue edges drift apart toward the sides, like a cheap lens. |
| **Saturation** | 1 = as drawn, 0 = grey, 2 = vivid. |
| **Contrast** | 1 = as drawn; more separates dark from bright. |
| **Depth of field** | Blurs what is far from the **Focus distance** (meters). **Blur amount** is how fast it blurs; **Blur limit** is the most blur. Draws the scene a second time, so it costs speed. |
| **Ambient occlusion** | Soft shadow in corners and creases. **Occlusion size** and **Occlusion strength** tune it. Draws the scene a second time, so it costs speed. |

Glow, depth of field and occlusion are the heavy ones on phones. Edge smoothing, vignette, fringe, saturation and contrast cost little.

## Scene variables

Values that exist only in this scene and **reset every time the scene starts** — e.g. a level timer. See [Variables](../logic/variables).

## Global variables

Values shared by **all scenes** and kept when you change scene — e.g. `Score`, `Lives`. New projects have a `Score` variable. Add with **+ Add variable**, choosing Number, Text or Yes / No.

## Touch controls

The on-screen controls shown while playing (also in [Project settings](./project-settings)):

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Joystick** | Show the virtual joystick. It drives the Platformer, Top-down and Car behaviors and the `joystickX` / `joystickY` values. |
| **Joystick side** | Left or Right. The buttons go on the other side. |
| **Buttons** | Round on-screen buttons. Each has a **Name** used by events, behaviors and scripts (e.g. `jump`), a **Label** shown on it (e.g. `A`), a **Color**, the **Keys** and **Gamepad** button that press it, and **On screen** (off = a keyboard / gamepad only button, e.g. `pause` on Esc). **Add button** adds one; 🗑 deletes it. Events can hide a button until it's useful and change its label with *Show*, *Hide* and *Set label* under **Input** — the same names as for UI buttons — see [getting into a boat](../behaviors#boat). |

<Side shot="controls-card" crop>

New projects have one button, `jump`, labelled **A**, pressed by **Space**, **J** and gamepad **A** — the Platformer behavior jumps with it (its **Jump button** setting names the button). WASD or the arrow keys and the left stick move.

**Keys:** tap **＋ Key**, then press the key on your keyboard (or pick one from the list on a phone); ✕ removes one. A button can have several keys, and the same key can press several buttons.

**Gamepad:** the button on a controller that presses it, or None.

A new button gets keys that suit its name — `use` / `interact`: **E** and **X**, `fire` / `shoot`: **F** and **RB**, `run` / `sprint`: **Shift** and **LB**, `pause`: **Esc** and **Start** — or the next free key (J, K, L…). Change them any time.

</Side>

**Gamepads** work in a browser and in exported games: the left stick or d-pad moves, and each button's **Gamepad** setting presses it. Every gamepad button also has its own name for *Key is held* / *On key pressed* and scripts: `PadA`, `PadB`, `PadX`, `PadY`, `PadLB`, `PadRB`, `PadLT`, `PadRT`, `PadStart`. The on-screen controls hide while a gamepad is used and come back on the next touch. The right stick turns a camera with [Mouse look](../behaviors#camera-follow).
