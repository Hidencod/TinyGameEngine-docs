# Street Racer

You against three AI drivers, three laps through a city built from free Kenney models. Everything in it is ordinary engine features: the **Car** behavior drives every car, **Camera follow** chases yours, and one event sheet runs the countdown, race positions, laps, the finish and your best time. There are no code scripts.

**Controls:** up = gas, down = brake and reverse, left / right = steer. On a computer use **WASD** or the **arrow keys**; on a phone, the joystick. You start last: finish first to win. Your best time is saved in the browser.

## Open it in the editor

1. Download **[street-racer.tge](https://hidencod.github.io/tge-assets/showcase/street-racer/street-racer.tge)** (5 MB, every model and sound inside).
2. Open the editor: **[editor.jeevagames.com](https://editor.jeevagames.com)** on a computer, or the Android app.
3. On the home screen tap **Import .tge** and pick the file. The project appears as *Street Racer (imported)*.
4. Open it and press **▶ Play**, or open **Logic → Main events** to read the race rules.

![Street Racer in the editor's desktop layout, with its event sheet open](/img/showcase/street-racer-events.webp)

The track, the city and the cars were placed by a small build script, but the result is a normal project: every road tile, barrier, building and waypoint is an object you can select, move or delete.

## How it works

### The racing line

An invisible box tagged **`#waypoint`** sits in the middle of the road on every road tile (and two more in each bend), all the way round. Each has an `index` variable (0, 1, 2…) so the order is clear. These waypoints are the **racing line**: the AI drivers follow it, and every car measures its progress along it.

The barriers are thin boxes with a **static** physics body, tagged `#wall`. The road itself is just flat model tiles: the barriers are what keeps the cars on track.

### One behavior, three kinds of driver

All four cars have the same [Car behavior](../behaviors#car) with **Path** set to `#waypoint`. Only their **Speed** and **Driver** differ:

| Car | Driver after the countdown | Speed (m/s) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Player | **player** | 26 |
| Rival Blue | **ai** | 25 |
| Rival Yellow | **ai** | 24 |
| Rival Green | **ai** | 23.5 |

They all start with Driver **none**, so nobody moves on the start screen. When you press *Race!*, the event sheet counts down 3, 2, 1 (with *Wait 1 seconds* between the numbers) and then uses **Set behavior setting** to switch your car to `player` and every `#ai` car to `ai`.

To make the race **easier or harder**, select a rival and change its Car **Speed**. Lower **Grip** makes them slide more in the bends.

### Race position in one event

Because the cars have a Path, each one keeps an instance variable **`progress`**: laps driven, as a decimal (`1.4` = one lap and 40% of the next). Your position is then simple:

- while racing, set `Place` to **1**,
- then **For each** `#ai` car whose `progress` is greater than `Player.progress`, add 1 to `Place`,
- and show `"P" + Place + " / 4"` in the corner.

The lap counter shows the car's `lap` variable the same way, and a **Trigger once while true** event shouts *FINAL LAP* when `Player.lap` reaches 3.

### Finish and best time

When `Player.progress` reaches **3**, the race is over:

- your car's Driver switches to `ai`, so it keeps driving by itself while the results show,
- a sub-event checks `Place`: 1 means *🏆 You win!* with confetti, anything else *You finished P2* (or P3, P4),
- if the time beats `BestTime` (or there is none yet), it is stored with **Save value**. On the next start, **Load value** reads it back. See [Saving between game sessions](../logic/variables#saving-between-game-sessions).

Bumping into a `#wall` plays a metal sound and shakes the camera a little; touching a rival plays a softer hit. **Race again** uses *Restart scene*.

## Make it yours

- Add a fifth car: duplicate a rival, give it another model and a different Speed. The position event already counts every `#ai` car; just change `/ 4` in the position text to `/ 5`.
- Make it longer: change the `3` in the finish event, the *FINAL LAP* event and the lap text to `5`.
- Reshape the track: move road tiles, barriers and waypoints together, keeping the waypoints in the middle of the road and their `index` in order.
- Swap the city: the buildings are models from the free library (**Assets → 🌐 Free models**).
