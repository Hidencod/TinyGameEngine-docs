# 2D lights

Sprites and tilemaps normally show their pictures as they are. Add a **Light 2D** to a scene and they are lit instead, as with Unity's 2D renderer:
- a dark cave lit by torches;
- a night street under lamps;
- a spotlight sweeping a prison yard.

![A cave room: a dim blue Global light, two torches and a cone from the ceiling, with shadows](/img/shots/2d-lights-game.webp)

## Add a light

**+ Add** → **Lights**:
- **Global Light 2D** lights everything the same. Use one, dim and a little blue, so the dark parts of a level stay visible.
- **Spot Light 2D** lights a circle round itself, or a cone. Put one on each torch, lamp or window.

As soon as a scene has a Light 2D, its sprites and tilemaps show **only** the light of its 2D lights: where no light reaches is black. Lights add up, so two torches side by side make a brighter patch.

3D meshes, particles and UI are not affected: 3D meshes keep using the [3D lights](./cameras-and-lights#lights).

## Light 2D settings

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Light Type** | **Global**: the whole scene. **Spot**: round the object (Unity's name for the round light). |
| **Color**, **Intensity** | The light's colour and how strong it is. Above 1 makes things brighter than their picture. |
| **Inner Radius** | Full light out to here (metres). |
| **Outer Radius** | No light from here on. Between the two, the light fades out. |
| **Inner Angle**, **Outer Angle** | 360 = all round. Lower makes a cone: full light inside the inner angle, fading out to the outer one. The cone points along the object's up: turn the object (rotation **Z**) to aim it, 180 points down. |
| **Falloff Strength** | How quickly the light fades between the radii: 0 stays bright to the edge, 1 fades fast. |
| **Shadows**, **Shadow Strength** | Shadow Caster 2D objects block the light (see below). At 1 none of this light gets into the shadow; at 0.5 half does. |
| **Volumetric Intensity** | The light also shows in the air, over everything: the haze round a torch or the beam of a lamp. 0 = off. |

![The Ceiling light selected: its cone (outer and inner) is drawn in the 2D view](/img/shots/2d-lights-editor.webp)

In the scene view each Spot light shows its outer reach (and its inner one, fainter) as a circle or a cone. The view draws the lights while you edit, so you see the scene as it will look.

## Shadows

1. Add a **Shadow Caster 2D** component to the objects that block light: walls, pillars, crates, the level's tilemap.
2. Switch on **Shadows** on the lights that should cast them.

A caster's shape is its collider's shape. For a tilemap that is its solid tiles; for a sprite, its collision polygon; for a shape, its box, circle or capsule. It doesn't need a collider, though: without one, the shape is what the collider would be.

**Self Shadows** (off by default): off, the light still reaches the caster itself and only what is behind it is dark, so a wall stays lit on the side facing the torch. On, the caster is in its own shadow too.

Shadows are sharp, like Unity's. The light picture is drawn at half the screen's pixels, which softens the edges a little.

## Unlit sprites and tilemaps

Some things should show as they are whatever the light: a glowing coin, a lit window, a sky backdrop. Set the Sprite's or Tilemap's **Material** to **Unlit** (Unity's *Sprite-Unlit-Default*). Lit is the default.

## In events and scripts

The settings can change while the game runs: a flickering torch, a lamp that switches off, a light that follows the hero.
- **Events:** **Set component setting** on the light's object (for example *light2d* → *intensity*). Moving the object moves its light.
- **Scripts:** `entity.component("light2d").intensity = 0.8 + Math.random() * 0.4`. An object's **Enabled** switch turns its light on and off.

## Good to know

- 2D lights work in the X–Y plane, which is how a side-on or top-down 2D game is laid out (see [2D games](./2d-games)).
- The light is worked out per pixel of the screen. Anything drawn where a shadow falls is darkened, even a sprite standing in front of the wall that casts it. Set things that must stay bright to **Unlit**.
- Each light with shadows is one more drawing pass a frame. A dozen torches are fine on a phone; hundreds are not.
- Exported games, the Android app and desktop builds draw 2D lights the same way.
