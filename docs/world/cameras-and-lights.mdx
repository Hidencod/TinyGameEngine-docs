# Cameras & lights

<Art name="star-small" size={85} />

## Camera

The game is shown through the **main camera**. Every new scene has one called *Camera*. What you see in the editor’s 3D view is **not** the game camera — press ▶ Play to see through it.

<Side shot="card-camera" crop>

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Main camera** | The game is shown through this camera. Only one camera should be main. |
| **Projection** | **Perspective (3D)** — far things look smaller. **Orthographic (flat)** — no perspective, like an isometric or 2D game. |
| **Field of view** | (Perspective) How wide it sees, 20–120°. 60 is normal; lower zooms in. |
| **View size** | (Orthographic) Half the height of the view, in meters. |
| **Near / Far** | Nothing closer than Near or farther than Far is drawn. |

</Side>

<Crop name="card-camera-ortho" caption="Orthographic camera" width={360} />

### Make the camera follow the player

Add the **Camera follow** behavior to the camera and set its **Target** to your player. See [Behaviors](../behaviors#camera-follow). Actions: **Shake camera**, **Camera look at**, **Set camera position**.

## Lights

Add lights from **＋ Add → Lights**. Every scene starts with a **Sun**. Change a light’s kind in its **Light** card.

<Row>
  <Crop name="card-light-directional" caption="☀️ Sun (directional)" width={300} />
  <Crop name="card-light-point" caption="💡 Lamp (point)" width={300} />
  <Crop name="card-light-spot" caption="🔦 Spotlight" width={300} />
  <Crop name="card-light-hemisphere" caption="🌤 Sky (hemisphere)" width={300} />
</Row>

| Kind | Like… | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **☀️ Sun (directional)** | Sunlight | Parallel rays from one direction; only its **rotation** matters. The main light of outdoor scenes. |
| **💡 Lamp (point)** | A light bulb | Shines in all directions from its position. **Range** limits how far (0 = unlimited). |
| **🔦 Spotlight** | A torch | A cone of light. **Cone angle** sets its width. |
| **🌤 Sky (hemisphere)** | Sky and ground bounce | Soft light: **Sky color** from above, **Ground color** from below. No shadows. Great fill light. |

Common fields: **Color**, **Brightness** (0–5), **Casts shadows**.

### Shadow cascades

A **Sun** that casts shadows has **Shadow cascades** (Off, 2, 3 or 4). Off is one shadow map that follows the camera: sharp up close, but it reaches only a short way. With cascades, the shadow is cut into several maps of growing size, so shadows stay sharp near the camera and still reach about 120 m, which suits big outdoor scenes. It works in the game only (the editor view keeps the single map), and only for one Sun per scene. Each cascade is one more shadow draw, so use **2** on phones; Low graphics quality (shadows off) turns cascades off with the rest.

:::tip[Lighting on phones]
Shadows are the most expensive part of drawing. Keep **one** shadow-casting light (the sun), turn shadows off on lamps, and use **Ambient light** (Scene tab) or a Sky light to brighten dark areas. You can turn all shadows off in [Project settings](../editor/project-settings).
:::

### Lens flare

A **Sun**, **Lamp** or **Spotlight** can have a **Lens flare**: a bright glow, a long thin streak and a string of soft rings across the screen when you look toward the light, like the sun in a camera lens. **Flare size** and **Flare strength** tune it (1 is normal).

- The flare is on the screen only when the light is in front of you, and it fades out toward the edge of the screen.
- It goes out when something solid is between the camera and the light (a wall, a hill, a building) and comes back softly when the way is clear. Glass, water and effects don't block it.
- A **Sun**'s flare sits where the sun would be in the sky, as far as the camera sees. A lamp's flare is at the lamp, and fades when the lamp is far away.
- It works in the game only: the editor view doesn't draw flares. It costs a few extra pictures on the screen, so it is cheap.
