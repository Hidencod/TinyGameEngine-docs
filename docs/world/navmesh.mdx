# Navmesh (pathfinding)

By default, characters that **go around walls** (the *Follow target* behavior with Pathfinding, and the **Move to position** block) search a flat grid on the floor they stand on. That is fine for a yard with walls and rocks, but it cannot climb a staircase to the next floor or jump a gap.

The **navmesh** can. Turn it on and the game works out, from your static colliders, every place a character of its size can stand: ramps, stairs, upper floors, the ground under a bridge. Paths are then searched over that.

## Turn it on

**Project settings → Navigation → Baked navmesh.**

| Setting | Means |
| --- | --- |
| **Cell size** | How finely the floor is sampled. Smaller is more exact and slower to bake (0.5 m is fine for most games). |
| **Steepest slope** | Ground steeper than this is not walkable. |
| **Step height** | Edges up to this high are stepped over (kerbs, stairs). |

The mesh is made **the first time a character needs a path** (a moment of work on a big level) and is kept until the scene ends. A mesh is made per character size, measured from the character's model, so a tall guard and a small dog get their own. Only **static** colliders count: moving bodies are not walls, and a wall that appears later is not seen until you call `game.navmesh.rebuild()`.

## What it understands

- **Slopes and stairs**: ground within the slope limit, joined by steps up to the step height.
- **Several floors**: floors above each other are separate; a character reaches the upper one only by walking up something (stairs, a ramp) or over a link. The ground under an upper floor stays walkable.
- **Room for the character**: places where its body would not fit (under a low ceiling, between close walls) are left out.
- **Goals off the mesh**: a goal in a wall or across a gap makes the character walk to the reachable spot closest to it.

## Walk to another floor

**Move to position** has an optional **Floor height Y**. Left empty, the character goes to a place on the floor it is on; give a height to pick another floor at the same X and Z. (In scripts: `entity.moveTo(x, z, speed, { y: 4 })`.) **Follow target** goes to wherever the target stands.

## Jumping across gaps: Nav link

A gap, a ledge down, a ladder: places the mesh does not join. Add a **Nav link** component to an object at the take-off spot and set **Lands on** to an object at the landing spot. Characters then jump from one to the other, on an arc, when that is the way. **Both ways** lets them jump back; **Reach** is how far from each object they may start and land.

## From scripts

```ts
const r = game.navmesh.findPath(enemy.position, player.position, { radius: 0.4, height: 1.8 });
if (r && r.complete) {
  for (const p of r.points) console.log(p.x, p.y, p.z, p.leap ? 'jump' : '');
}
const floor = game.navmesh.sample(player.position);   // nearest place to stand
game.navmesh.rebuild();                               // after walls changed
```

## See the mesh

**In the editor:** **Gizmos → Navmesh** in the scene view's toolbar draws the walkable area as green cells on your floors, before you press Play. It follows what you move: walls cut holes, steps and slopes join or split areas. It is made from the objects' shapes, so it is a close picture, not the exact mesh the game builds. The tops of cubes show as separate green islands when they are too high to step onto.

**In the game:** Press **Play**, then the **Show colliders** button. The places a character can walk are drawn as **green cells** on the floor, with the colliders outlined. A wall that has no green hole under it and no outline has **no collider**, or it is not **static**: add a **Collider** and set the **Rigidbody** to *Static* (or leave out the Rigidbody) and it blocks paths.

This is the same idea as Unity's NavMesh display. Unity bakes the objects you mark **Navigation Static** and agents never leave that surface; here the objects that count are the static colliders, and the green cells are the baked surface.

A goal that is off the mesh (the player is outside the ground, or inside a wall) sends the character to the nearest place it can stand, and it stops there. It does not walk off the ground in a straight line.

## Good to know

- With the navmesh off, nothing changes: the grid search is used.
- If the mesh cannot be made (no static colliders), characters fall back to the grid search.
- Not there yet: baking ahead of time, avoiding other moving characters, different surface costs (mud, road).
