## Get sounds

<Side shot="sounds-library">

**Assets → 🔊 Free sounds** opens 27 game sounds by Kenney (CC0, free for any game), grouped as **Rewards** (Coin, PowerUp, Bonus, Win, Lose), **Actions** (Jump, Laser, Zap…), **Hits** (Explosion, Punch, GlassBreak…), menu clicks and engine hums.

- Tap **▶** to listen.
- **＋ Add** puts it in your project; **Use** means it is already there.

</Side>


## Make a sound

<Side shot="sound-maker">

**Free sounds → 🎛 Make a sound** (or **Files → ＋ New → Sound**) makes a retro sound effect, the kind 8-bit games use, right in the editor.

1. Pick a preset: **Coin**, **Jump**, **Laser**, **Explosion**, **Hit**, **Power-up**, **Blip**, **Click**, **Win** or **Lose**. It plays at once.
2. **🎲** makes another take on the same preset. Tap it until one sounds right.
3. Change it if you like (it plays again each time you let go of a slider).
4. Give it a name and tap **Save**. It is in your project's sounds, ready for *Play sound*.

</Side>

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Square / Saw / Triangle / Sine / Noise** | The tone: square is classic chiptune, saw is buzzy, triangle and sine are soft, noise hisses (explosions, hits, wind). |
| **Pitch** | How high it starts. For noise, how rough: low rumbles, high hisses. |
| **Slide to** | The pitch it ends on. Higher rises (a jump), lower falls (a laser, a game over). The same as **Pitch** stays on one note. |
| **Length** | How long it stays at full volume. |
| **Fade** | How long it takes to fade out after that. |
| **Wobble** | A vibrato: the pitch wobbles up and down. |
| **Volume** | How loud it is. |

The picture beside ▶ shows the sound, with its length in seconds.

The sound maker keeps its settings with the sound. To change one later, tap it in **Files** and choose **Edit sound**. **Save** then changes it in place, so every *Play sound* and Sound component using it plays the new one. Sounds [Claude](../editor/claude-link) makes open the same way.

## Make a beat

**Free sounds → 🥁 Make a beat** (or **Files → ＋ New → Beat**) opens the beat maker, laid out like FL Studio: a **Rack** of channels with step pads, a **piano roll** for tunes, a **Playlist** that puts patterns together into a song, and a **Mixer**. Everything plays while you change it, so you hear each change on the next pass.

### The rack

A new beat starts with a kick on every beat, claps on 2 and 4, hats in between, an empty Snare row and a Bass line.

- **Tap a step** to turn it on or off. A bar is 16 steps (16th notes), in groups of four, one group per beat.
- **▶** plays the pattern over and over; the step that is sounding lights up.
- **The dot** at the start of a row mutes it. **Hold the dot** (or right-click it) to **solo** the row: only it plays, so you can hear it alone. Hold it again to hear everything. Solo is only for listening: it isn't saved with the beat.
- **+ Channel** adds a row: a drum (**Kick**, **Snare**, **Clap**, **Hat**, **Open hat**, **Tom**, **Rim**, **Crash**), one from the second kit (🪘 **808**, **Punch kick**, **Tight snare**, **Snap**, **Shaker**, **Cowbell**, **Conga**, **Ride**), a synth (**Bass**, **Lead**, **Pluck**, **Pad**, **Keys**, **Chip**), a sound from your project, or a free sound.
- **Tap a row's name** for its menu: **Solo**, **Piano roll**, **Step pads**, **Sound…** (change what it plays), **Instrument…**, **Mixer**, **Rename**, **Duplicate**, **Clear** and **Delete**.

<Side shot="beat-velocity">

**Velocity** (above the rows) shows how hard each step hits, as a bar. Drag up and down on a row's steps to make them softer or louder: soft hats between loud ones give a beat its groove. A drag changes the steps that are on (and the first one you press, to turn on a single step); at the very bottom a step goes off. Tap **Velocity** again for the normal pads, where a softer step shows paler.

</Side>

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **BPM** | The tempo: beats per minute (− and + change it by one). |
| **Swing** | Makes every second step a little late, for a shuffle or hip-hop feel. |
| **🔁** | On: saved as a seamless loop (what rings on past the end comes in at the start, so it repeats without a gap). Off: saved once, ringing out at the end (a jingle). |
| **↶ ↷** | Undo and redo inside the beat maker. |

### Instruments

<Side shot="beat-instrument">

**Instrument…** in a row's menu shapes its sound. You hear it each time you let go of a slider.

| Synths | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Attack** | How long a note takes to get loud: short for a punch, long for a soft swell. |
| **Decay** | How long it then takes to fall to the sustain level. |
| **Sustain** | How loud it stays while the note is held (0 = it fades away even when held, like a pluck). |
| **Release** | How long it rings on after the note ends. |
| **Tone** | Dark (low) to bright (high). |
| **Tune** | Shifts every note up or down, in semitones (12 = an octave). |
| **Wave** | The oscillator: the synth's own voice (its name), or a plain **Saw**, **Square**, **Tri** (triangle) or **Sine**. |
| **Octave** | Plays every note one or two octaves higher or lower. |
| **Unison** | Plays each note with up to 5 voices at once, spread apart by **Detune**: thick and wide. A saw with 5 voices and some detune is the "supersaw" of trance and EDM. |
| **Glide** | Each note slides from the pitch of the one before, taking this long: the rubbery 303 and 808 bass lines. **Off** jumps straight there. |
| **LFO** | A slow wobble. **To** picks what it moves: the **Pitch** (vibrato), the **Filter** (a wobble bass, at a **Rate** of 2-4 Hz) or the **Volume** (tremolo). **Depth** is how much; **Off** turns it off. |

Drums and sounds have **Tune**, **Tone**, **Attack** and **Length** (how long it plays: **Full**, or cut short for a tight, dry hit).

</Side>

### Patterns

A **pattern** is a bar or a few of every channel's steps and notes: a beat, a fill, a chorus. The bar under the tabs shows the pattern you are editing.

- **The pattern menu** switches patterns. **＋** makes a new, empty one.
- **⋯** renames, duplicates, clears or deletes it.
- **1 bar / 2 bars / 4 bars** is the pattern's length (16, 32 or 64 steps).

The channels are the same in every pattern; each pattern has its own steps and notes for them. To make a variation, duplicate a pattern and change the copy.

### The piano roll

**Piano roll** in a row's menu opens its notes in the current pattern (a row in the piano roll shows them small in place of its steps: tap them to open it again). Up is higher, and each column is a step.

- **Tap** an empty spot to put a note in. Drag while you put it in to make it longer.
- **Drag a note** to move it (higher, lower, earlier, later), or **drag its end** to make it longer or shorter.
- **Tap a note** to take it out.
- **1/16 … 1** is how long a new note is (a step, an eighth, a quarter, a half or a whole bar); it follows the last note you stretch.
- **Tap a key** on the left to hear that pitch.
- **Two fingers** scroll.
- **← Rack** goes back.

A drum or sound in the piano roll plays higher or lower with each note, so a single sound can become a tune. The other channels' notes show faintly behind, so a melody can follow the bass.

**✏ Draw** is the tool above. **⬚ Select** picks notes to change together:

- **Tap a note** to select it, or **drag a box** around some. Drag a selected note to move them all.
- **All** selects every note; **Copy** and **Paste** copy them to the same steps in another pattern or channel (or another beat); **⧉** repeats them right after themselves; **↑ ↓** move them up or down; **🗑** deletes them.

**♬ Chord** (in Draw) puts a whole chord in with each tap, on the note you tap: **Triad (scale)** and **7th (scale)** build it from the scale's notes (so in A minor, tapping A gives A minor and tapping C gives C major), or pick a fixed **Major**, **Minor**, **Diminished**, **Sus2**, **Sus4**, **7**, **Maj7**, **Min7**, **Power** (root, fifth, octave) or **Octave**. Dragging stretches the whole chord. **Single notes** turns it off.

**⋯** changes the selected notes (in Select), or all of the channel's notes in the pattern:

| Tool | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Arpeggiate** | Plays each chord one note after another through its length: **Up**, **Down** or **Up and down**, at **1/16**, **1/8** or **1/4**. Single notes stay. |
| **Quantize** | Moves notes to the nearest 1/8, 1/4, 1/2 or whole bar. |
| **Humanize** | Varies each note's velocity a little, as a player would. |
| **Legato** | Holds each note until the next one starts. |

**Vel**, along the bottom, has a bar for each note: drag up and down on it to make notes softer or louder (in Select, only the selected ones).

**Scale** picks a key and scale (**Major**, **Minor**, **Dorian**, **Pentatonic**, **Minor pentatonic**, **Blues**). The notes outside it are shaded and the key's note is marked, and new and moved notes land on the scale, so every note fits. **↑ ↓** then move along the scale. **Chromatic** turns it off.

### The playlist

<Side shot="beat-playlist">

The **Playlist** is the song: patterns placed in time, one bar per column, like FL Studio's playlist.

- **Tap an empty spot** to put the current pattern there. Pick another pattern in the pattern menu to place that one.
- **Drag a clip** to move it to another bar or track.
- **Drag a clip's right edge** to make it longer or shorter, in quarter bars. Longer repeats the pattern (lines mark each repeat), so one 1-bar drum pattern can run under a whole verse; shorter cuts it off.
- **Tap a clip** for **Edit pattern** (opens it in the rack), **Duplicate** (a copy right after it), **Make unique** (a copy only this clip plays, to change it alone), **Pattern length** (after a resize) and **Remove**.
- Clips on different tracks play together: drums on one track and a melody pattern on another, say.
- **▶** in the playlist plays the song; in the rack and piano roll it plays the current pattern.

**The ruler** along the top (the bar numbers):

- **Tap a bar number** to play the song from that bar. The ▲ marks where ▶ starts from.
- **Drag along the ruler** to loop those bars: they play over and over, so you can work on a part. **🔁 2–3 ✕** above shows the loop; tap it to play the whole song again. Like solo, the loop is only for listening; Done saves the whole song.

**↻** (when the beat loops) picks the bar the loop starts again from: a song with an **intro**. Pick **Bar 3** and bars 1-2 play once, then bar 3 to the end repeats; a dashed green line marks it. **Bar 1** loops all of it. The game plays it the same way.

**⬚ Select** picks clips: tap them, or drag a box across empty space. Drag one to move them all together. **All** picks every clip, **⧉** copies them right after themselves (a 4-bar section becomes 8 bars), **🗑** removes them. **✏ Draw** goes back to placing clips.

With an empty playlist, the song is the current pattern.

</Side>

### The mixer

<Side shot="beat-mixer">

The **Mixer** has a strip for each channel and one for the whole song (**Master**). Changes are heard at once.

| Channel | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Volume** | How loud the channel is. |
| **Pan** | Left or right. |
| **Filter** | Cuts the highs above this (muffled, far away, underwater); **Open** lets everything through. |
| **Resonance** | Makes the filter ring at its cutoff: the classic squelchy synth sound. |
| **Reverb** | How much of the channel goes into the shared reverb (a room around it). |
| **Delay** | How much goes into the shared echo. |
| **Drive** | Distortion: warm and fuzzy at a little, crunchy and loud at a lot. Good on bass and leads. |
| **Pump** | Sidechain: the channel ducks every time a kick (**Kick**, **Punch kick** or **808**) hits and swells back up, the pumping sound of house and EDM. Try it on pads and bass. |

**M** and **S** by a channel's name mute and solo it.

| Master | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Volume** | The whole song. |
| **Room** | The reverb's size, from a small room to a long hall. |
| **Delay** | The echo's timing, in step with the tempo: **1/8**, **3/16** (dotted eighth) or **1/4**. |
| **Feedback** | How many times the echo repeats. |

</Side>

### Layers (adaptive music)

<Side shot="beat-layers">

A beat can be saved in **layers** that the game turns up and down while it plays, all in time: calm music while the player explores, the drums coming in when an enemy sees them, a danger melody on top in a fight. It's the vertical layering of FMOD and Wwise, made in the beat maker.

- **🧅** on a channel's mixer card, or **Layer** in its menu in the rack, puts it in a layer: one there is, or **New layer…** with a name (*Drums*, *Danger*…). **None** takes it out.
- Channels in no layer always play.
- The chips at the top of the **Mixer** turn each layer off and on, to hear what the game will do. Like solo, that's only for listening: the beat saves with every layer.

**Done** saves the beat as usual, and each layer as well (they don't show in **Files**: they go with the beat). The layers together sound exactly like the beat; each one takes as much space as the beat again.

In the game, play the beat with **Play music** and fade layers with **Set music layer** (see [Music](#music)).

</Side>

### Saving

Tap **Done** and name it: the song (or the pattern, with an empty playlist) becomes a sound in your project. Play it with *Play sound*, with **Loop** on for background music. A bar at 120 BPM is 2 seconds; saved as OGG (see [Sound files](#sound-files)) an 8-bar song is about 100-300 KB.

To change it later, tap it in **Files** and choose **Edit beat**. **Done** changes the sound in place, so everything using it plays the new one.

### With Claude

Through the [Claude link](../editor/claude-link), Claude can make beats and songs in the beat maker, and read and change the ones you made. Each is a normal beat: open it with **Edit beat** and carry on by hand.

- *Make a fast chiptune loop for the boss fight, 160 BPM.*
- *Make a calm 8-bar song for the menu in C major: soft keys, a bass line, and a drum fill at the end.*
- *Make the hats in my Groove beat softer and add a clap on 2 and 4.*
- *Make a house loop at 124 BPM: punch kick, an 808 bass line with glide, and supersaw chords pumping to the kick.*
- *Make boss music at 140 BPM with a 2-bar intro that plays once, then loops.*
- *Make adaptive music for the forest level: a calm base, a Drums layer and a Danger layer, and bring the drums in when an enemy is near the player.*

Claude can't hear: listen with ▶ and say what to change.

## Make music

<Side shot="music-maker">

**Free sounds → 🎹 Make music** (or **Files → ＋ New → Music**) opens a music editor: [BeepBox](https://www.beepbox.co), a free song maker for chiptune-style game music, built into the editor (it works offline).

- **The grid:** tap to put a note in, tap it again to take it out, drag one to make it longer. Up is higher.
- **The numbers below it:** the song from left to right, one row per channel (the top rows are tunes, the bottom one drums). Each number is a pattern: tap a box to edit that part of the song, and give two boxes the same number to repeat a pattern.
- **Play** listens to the song from the start.
- **Instrument Settings** choose each channel's sound (**Type**: piano, synth, guitar, drums…) and **Customize Instrument** changes it further.
- **Song Settings** set the **Tempo** (speed), **Key**, **Scale** and **Rhythm**.

Tap **Done** and name it: the song becomes a sound in your project, played once from start to end. It ends at the last bar with notes in it (a new song is 16 bars long, mostly empty, and the empty end is left out). Play it with *Play sound* and **Loop** on for background music.

</Side>

To change it later, tap it in **Files** and choose **Edit music**. **Done** changes the sound in place, so everything using it plays the new one.

:::tip[Keep songs short]
A song is saved as a sound file (OGG: about 200 KB per 30 seconds; WAV: about 2 MB). A short loop with **Loop** on sounds the same in a game as a long song and keeps the game small. The song's length is in **Edit → Change Song Length**.
:::

**Your own sounds:** **Assets → ⬆ Import** an MP3, OGG, WAV or M4A. Preview any sound from its menu in the Assets tab (**Preview sound**); tap the player bar at the bottom to stop it.

## Play a sound from an event

The simplest way: **Add action → Audio → Play sound**, choose the sound, a **volume** and whether it **loops**. It can also play through a **mixer group**, at another **pitch** (1 = normal, 1.2 = a little higher and faster; a small random pitch like `random(0.9, 1.1)` keeps repeated sounds from sounding the same) and **fade in**. **Stop sound** stops one sound or all of them, with an optional **fade out**. **Is sound playing** checks whether one is still going.

Example — a coin sound: *WHEN Player collides with Coin → Audio: Play sound `Coin`*.

## The Sound component

For a sound that belongs to an object — an engine hum on a car, a waterfall, background music — add the **Sound** component:

<Side shot="card-audio" crop>

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Sound file** | The sound asset (or pick a free one). |
| **Play on start** | Starts when the scene starts. |
| **Loop** | Repeats forever. |
| **Volume** | 0–1. |
| **Pitch** | 1 = normal; 2 = an octave up and twice as fast. |
| **Mixer group** | The group of the [audio mixer](#the-audio-mixer) it plays through (Master = none). |
| **3D sound** | Quieter when the object is far from the camera, and from the side it is on. |
| **Full volume within** | (3D) Closer than this, it plays at full volume. |
| **Heard up to** | (3D) How far it carries. |
| **Fade with distance** | (3D) *Natural* fades like real sound and never quite goes silent; *Linear* reaches silence at **Heard up to**. |

Actions **Play its sound** / **Stop its sound** (with a fade out) control it.

</Side>

:::info[Sound on phones]
Browsers only allow sound after the player touches the screen. Exported games show a *Tap to play* screen that unlocks sound; in the editor the ▶ Play tap does it.
:::

## Music

**Play music** plays one track at a time: starting another **cross-fades** from the one playing over the time you give (1 s by default), and asking for the track that is already playing only changes its volume. **Stop music** fades it out; **Is music playing** checks it. Use it for level music, a menu theme, or a boss fight that takes over.

**Set music layer** fades one [layer of a beat](#layers-adaptive-music) to a volume (0 = off, 1 = full) over the time you give, while the music goes on in time. A layer set before the music starts starts that way: set *Danger* to 0 just before **Play music**, and the music starts calm. A layer keeps its volume until you change it, from track to track.

| Event | Set music layer |
| --- | --- |
| On start of scene | *Danger* to 0 (fade 0), then **Play music** *Forest* |
| Enemy is near the player (distance under 10) | *Danger* to 1 (fade 2) |
| Else | *Danger* to 0 (fade 3) |

### In time with the music

A beat knows its tempo, so the game can keep to its beat. For other music (imported, or from the music editor) set it in **Files → Tempo** (in beats a minute; 4 beats to a bar).

- **Play music** with **In time with the music** set to **On the next bar** (or beat) starts the new track there, and the one playing fades out from there: going from explore to fight music lands on the beat instead of cutting in mid-note. Use tracks at the same tempo.
- **Play sound** has the same choice: a sting or jingle (an enemy spotted, a level up) waits for the next beat or bar so it fits the music.
- **On music beat** is true once on each beat of the music (or every 2nd, 4th… from the beat you give), and **On music bar** once on each bar: lights that pulse, enemies that hop or shoot on the beat, a rhythm game.
- `musicbeat()` counts the beats since the music started (0, 1, 2 …; -1 with no music that has a tempo), `musicbar()` the bars, `musictime()` the seconds, `musicbpm()` the tempo. `musicbeat() % 4` is the beat in the bar (0-3).

| Event | Actions |
| --- | --- |
| On music beat | *Lights*: Set scale to 1.2, then Tween scale to 1 in 0.2 s |
| Enemy sees the player, Trigger once while true | **Play music** *Fight* (on the next bar) |
| On music beat (every 2) | *Turret* spawns *Bullet* |

When the music changes on a bar, the beats count from 0 again for the new track (the old one keeps counting until then).

## Sound files

Sounds you make (the sound maker, the beat maker, the music editor and Claude) are saved as **OGG**, compressed about 10 to 20 times smaller than a WAV and sounding the same in a game, as the free sounds are. Loops stay seamless.

**Project settings → Audio mixer → Saved sounds** picks how:

| Saved sounds | What it gives |
| --- | --- |
| **OGG, smaller** | The smallest files; fine for effects and busy music. |
| **OGG, balanced** | The default: small, and clear for music. |
| **OGG, best** | About twice the size of balanced, for music with quiet, detailed parts. |
| **WAV** | Not compressed (about 10 times bigger). |

Sounds already in the project (imported WAVs, or made before) stay as they are:

- **Compress…** in a sound's menu in **Files** makes that one OGG, at the quality you pick. Everything using it plays the new file.
- **🗜 Compress all sounds** under **Saved sounds** does every sound that isn't OGG yet, at the project's quality. It shows how many there are and their size, and goes away when there are none.

Compressing keeps a made sound's settings: **Edit beat**, **Edit music** and **Edit sound** still open it, and saving there makes it again.

## The audio mixer

**Project settings → Audio mixer** holds the game's **groups**, like Unity's Audio Mixer: for example *Music*, *SFX* and *Voices*. A sound plays through the group you pick (in **Play sound**, **Play music** or the Sound component); a group sets the volume of everything in it.

| Group setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Name** | What sounds and events call it. Renaming it renames it on every Sound component. |
| **On** | Off mutes the group. |
| **Volume** | 0–1, under the master volume. |
| **Low-pass** | Muffles the group (in Hz; 0 = off). 500–800 sounds like being under water or behind a wall. |

While playing:

| Block | Does |
| --- | --- |
| **Set group volume** | A group's volume, faded over the time you give. `Master` is the master volume. An options menu's music slider is one of these. |
| **Mute group** | Silence a group, or bring it back. |
| **Set group low-pass** | Muffle a group (fade it in when the player dives, out when they come up). |
| **Pause all sounds** / **Resume all sounds** | For a pause menu: everything stops where it is and carries on later. |

## In scripts

```js
const hit = audio.play('Punch', { group: 'SFX', pitch: random(0.9, 1.1) })
const engine = audio.play('Engine', { loop: true, fadeIn: 0.5 })
engine.pitch = 0.8 + speed / 20        // change it while it plays
engine.stop(1)                          // fade out over 1 s

audio.playMusic('Boss', { fade: 2 })     // cross-fade from the level music
audio.setMusicLayer('Danger', 1, 2)      // a layer of the music in, over 2 s
audio.playMusic('Fight', { sync: 'bar' })  // on the next bar
audio.play('Sting', { sync: 'beat' })      // on the next beat
if (audio.musicBeat % 4 === 0) { }        // the first beat of a bar
audio.setGroupVolume('Music', 0.3, 1)    // duck the music for a cutscene
audio.setGroupLowpass('SFX', 600, 0.3)   // under water
audio.pauseAll()
```

`audio.play()` returns the sound: `volume`, `pitch`, `playing`, `stop(fadeOut)` and `fadeTo(volume, seconds)`. Also `audio.stop(tag, fadeOut)`, `audio.isPlaying(tag)`, `audio.music`, `audio.stopMusic(fade)`, `audio.getMusicLayer(layer)`, `audio.musicBeat`, `audio.musicBar`, `audio.musicTime`, `audio.musicBpm`, `audio.getGroupVolume(group)`, `audio.setGroupMuted(group, muted)`, `audio.resumeAll()`, and `entity.stopSound(fadeOut)`.
