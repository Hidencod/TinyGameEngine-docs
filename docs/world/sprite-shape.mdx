# Sprite Shape

Tiles are square. For rolling hills, round floating islands or a bendy ledge, draw the ground along a **curve** instead. A **Sprite Shape** (Unity's Sprite Shape) puts pictures on the curve's outline:
- the **fill** covers the inside of a closed curve, with a colour or a repeated picture;
- **edge** pictures run along the outline, and which one is used depends on the way each part faces: grass where it faces up, a rock rim on the sides and below;
- with a **Collider**, the collider follows the outline, so players and balls walk and roll along the hills.

![A hilly island, a floating island and an open ledge drawn with Sprite Shapes; wheels roll down the slopes](/img/shots/sprite-shape-game.webp)

The [Sprocket Hills](../showcase/sprocket-hills) showcase is a whole game made this way: rolling pixel-art hills, a floating ledge and parallax far hills.

## Add one

**Objects → ＋ Add**, under **Sprites**: **Sprite Shape**. You get a closed curve filled with a colour, and a Collider. Tap **2D** in the 3D view's toolbar, then drag the curve's points to shape the ground: double-tap the line to add a point, select a point and press Delete to remove one (see [Curve](./curve)).

A Sprite Shape uses the **Curve** on the same object, in its X / Y plane (Z is ignored). An object without one shows **＋ Curve** in the Sprite Shape card.

## Fill

**Fill** is **Colour**, **Picture** or **None**. A picture repeats across the shape; **Fill size** is how many metres one picture covers. **Fill normal map** gives the picture bumps for [2D lights with Normal Maps](./2d-lights#normal-maps). Only a **closed** curve has an inside to fill (turn on **Closed loop** in the Curve card).

## Edges

![The Island selected: its curve points, fill picture and two edge ranges](/img/shots/sprite-shape-editor.webp)

Each **edge range** is a picture and two angles. The angle is the way a part of the outline faces: **0** = up, **90** = right, **180** = down, **−90** (or 270) = left. A part uses the first range that holds its angle, so:

| Range | Picture |
| --- | --- |
| −55 … 55 | grass (the parts facing up and a bit to the sides) |
| 55 … 305 | earth rim (the sides and the bottom) |

**＋ Edge range** adds one; 🗑 removes one. A part that no range holds has no edge. Each range with a picture can also have a **Normal map** (laid out like its picture) for [2D lights with Normal Maps](./2d-lights#normal-maps); its bumps turn with the outline.

- An edge picture is drawn **sideways along the line**: its **top** faces out, its bottom faces in, and it repeats along the line at its own width / height. A strip of grass cut from a tile works well.
- **Edge height** is how thick the strip is, in metres.
- **Edge inside** is how much of the strip lies inside the outline: 0 = all outside, 0.5 = centred on the line, 1 = all inside.

An **open** curve (Closed loop off) has no inside: only its edges are drawn, and its outside is **up** when the line runs left to right. That makes a ledge or a vine bridge.

### More pictures per range

**＋** on a range's angle row adds another picture to it (picture 2, 3…). The first picture is used unless a curve point picks another (see [Points](#points)): flowers on one hilltop, a cracked stone on one wall.

## Corners

![A floating block with a step: rounded grass over the outer corners, the wall's rim meeting the grass at the inner one](/img/shots/sprite-shape-corners.webp)

Where the outline turns sharply, edges meet at an angle. **Corner pictures** cover the join, like Unity's corner sprites. A corner is drawn at a curve point when:
- the outline turns there by at least **Corner angle** degrees (30 by default);
- both sides have an edge;
- one side faces up or down and the other left or right;
- the Sprite Shape has a picture for that kind of corner.

| Corner | Where |
| --- | --- |
| **Outer top left / right** | the top meets a side, and the shape sticks out (the top corners of a block) |
| **Outer bottom left / right** | the bottom meets a side, sticking out |
| **Inner top left / right** | a top meets a side going up (the foot of a step or a pit wall) |
| **Inner bottom left / right** | a bottom meets a side, going in |

Left and right name the way the side faces: an **inner top left** corner is where a floor meets a wall that faces left.

A corner picture is drawn upright over a square **Edge height** across, and the edges on both sides stop at its sides. Draw it like the edge pictures: for an outer top right corner, the grass top of the edge picture rounded over to the right.

:::tip
Corners need sharp points. Smooth curves bend through their points, so they never turn sharply at one. Use **Catmull-Rom** with **Tension** 0 (straight lines between the points), or **Bezier** with the point's handles set to **Sharp corner**.
:::

## Points

![The Block's point 5 with its Edge set to None: the lower step has no grass, and its inner corner went with it](/img/shots/sprite-shape-point.webp)

Each curve point has settings for the **segment that starts at it** (from it to the next point). Pick a point in the Scene view (or type its number in **Point**) to change them:

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Edge** | **Auto** picks the range by angle. **None** leaves the segment bare. **Edge 1, 2…** uses that range whichever way the segment faces. |
| **Picture** | Which of the range's pictures (shown when a range has more than one). |
| **Corner** | Off: no corner picture at this point. |
| **Height** | The edge's thickness here, times **Edge height**. It blends to the next point's height along the segment. |

The settings stay with their points when you add, remove or subdivide points.

## Look

- **Tint** and **Opacity** colour the pictures.
- **Order in layer** draws it in front of (higher) or behind (lower) sprites and tilemaps where they overlap.
- **Filter**: **Smooth** blends the pictures; **Pixel** keeps hard pixels for pixel art.
- **Material**: **Lit** shows [2D lights](./2d-lights); **Unlit** leaves it as it is.
- **Detail** is how many points each part of the curve is cut into; more is smoother.

## Collider

Give the object a **Collider** with the shape **Auto** (the menu item does). Its outline is the curve's:
- **Offset** moves the collider's edge out (+) or in (−) from the line, to match where the grass looks solid;
- an **open** curve's collider is a band under the line, **Thickness** metres thick;
- **Depth** is how deep it is along Z.

A Sprite Shape with a **Shadow Caster 2D** blocks [2D lights](./2d-lights#shadows) with the same outline.

## Coming from Unity

Unity's Sprite Shape Profile holds the angle ranges, their sprite variants, the corners and the fill; here they are on the component itself. Unity's control point settings (Sprite Variant, Corner, Height) are the **Point** section. The spline is the object's Curve, so its points, handle types and Closed loop setting are the Curve card's.
