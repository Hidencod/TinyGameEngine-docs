# Terrain

<Art name="penguin" size={95} />

A **terrain** is ground with hills: a square of land you shape by dragging on it, a bit like sculpting clay. It paints itself — grass where it's flat, rock on steep slopes, snow up high if you want, each with its own surface texture — and everything that walks, rolls or drives follows its hills.

## Add a terrain

**Objects → ＋ Add → Terrain (hills)**. It starts with gentle random hills, 40 × 40 m, centred in the scene, and the camera zooms out to show all of it.

New projects also have a flat **Ground**; delete it if the terrain replaces it, or keep it as a floor around the hills.

## Shape it

<Side shot="card-terrain" crop>

Select the terrain, open **Inspect** and tap **🖌️ Sculpt**. Then drag on the terrain in the 3D view:

| Tool | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Raise** | Pushes the ground up under the brush. Holding still keeps raising. |
| **Lower** | Digs it down (valleys, lakes, paths). |
| **Smooth** | Rounds off bumps and sharp edges. |
| **Flatten** | Levels the ground to the height where the drag started — roads, building plots, a flat arena on a hill. |
| **🎨 Paint** | Paints a surface onto the ground with a soft edge: a dirt path, a sandy beach, a cobbled road, rock or snow patches, more grass. Pick a surface (**Grass, Rock, Dirt, Snow, Stones, Sand**), each with its own texture, and change its **Tint** for any color (dark grass, red rock…). **Auto** erases paint back to the automatic look. |
| **🌳 Scatter** | Sprinkles copies of a 3D model over the ground under the brush — a forest, rocks, grass tufts, flowers. See below. |

**Brush size** is the radius in meters; **Strength** is how fast it works. A yellow ring shows where the brush is. Each drag is one step of **Undo**.

While sculpting, one finger (or the left mouse button) sculpts, two fingers (or the right mouse button) move and zoom the camera. Tap **Stop sculpting** to turn the camera again.

</Side>

Painting changes only the colors, not the shape, and stays when you reshape or change **Detail**. **🧽 Clear paint** removes all of it.

### Paint with your own images {/* #images */}

Next to the built-in surfaces, **＋ Image** adds a surface made from a picture: one already in your **Assets**, a new one from your phone's gallery or your computer (**Import image**), or one of the **Free textures** (lawn, forest floor, gravel, sand, rock, cobblestones, stone slabs, wood planks, blue tiles). It gets its own button with the picture on it; paint with it like any other surface.

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Tint** | White keeps the picture's own colors; any other color tints it. |
| **Image size** | How many meters one copy of the picture covers before it repeats. |
| **🗑 Remove image** | Takes it off the list; ground painted with it turns to dirt (Undo brings it back). |

A terrain can have up to **4** image surfaces. Use **seamless** textures (made to repeat without visible edges) — the free ones are. A normal photo shows a seam every time it repeats. Big photos are scaled down to 2048 pixels when you import them, so they stay light on phones.

### Scatter trees, rocks and grass {/* #scatter */}

With **🌳 Scatter**, pick a **Model** (any 3D model in the project — add one first from **Objects → ＋ Add → Free models library…**) and drag over the terrain. Copies stand on the ground with a random turn and size, and skip slopes too steep to stand on.

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Density** | How many per 100 m². Going over the same spot again only fills gaps — it never piles them up. |
| **Size from / to** | Each copy gets a random size in this range. |
| **Solid** | Copies get a Collider and block the player and [pathfinding](../behaviors#follow) — on for trees and rocks, off for grass and flowers. |
| **Remove** | The brush removes scattered copies instead of adding them. **🗑 Remove all scattered** clears them all. |

Scattered copies are ordinary objects inside the terrain (they move with it) and are tagged `#scattered`. The terrain's row in the Objects list folds when you scatter, so they don't flood it — tap its ▸ to see them. Each drag is one Undo step. Put `#scattered` in the camera's **Ignore tag** ([Camera follow](../behaviors#camera-follow)) so a forest doesn't keep pushing the camera in.

:::tip
Every copy is a real object: a few hundred are fine, thousands slow phones down. Grass and flowers look good at a low density spread over a big area.
:::

**⛰️ Make hills** replaces the terrain with new random hills up to **Hill height** meters (Undo brings the old ones back); **Flatten all** makes it flat again.

## Settings

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Size** | Width and depth in meters. Making it bigger stretches the same hills. |
| **Detail** | Squares per side: Low (32), Medium (64) or High (128). More detail = smaller bumps possible, but more work for the phone. Changing it keeps the shape. |
| **Grass / Rock / Snow** | The colors it paints with. Rock goes on steep slopes. |
| **Snow above** | Snow covers the ground above this height (0 = no snow). |
| **Textures** | Surface detail over the colors: grass blades and clumps, rough rock on steep slopes, soft snow, and the textures of painted surfaces. The colors still decide the look. Switch it off for plain colors (pictures you paint with **＋ Image** still show). |
| **Texture size** | How many meters one repeat of the detail covers (default 4). Smaller = finer detail. |

## Physics

A terrain comes with a **Collider** (shape *Auto*), which follows the hills exactly: players walk up them, balls roll down, cars drive over them. Don't give it a dynamic Rigid body — a terrain stays put.

[Pathfinding](../behaviors#follow) treats a terrain as ground to walk on, not as a wall. Steep slopes are still climbed only as far as the physics allows.

:::tip
Keep **Detail** at Medium unless you need small bumps: a High terrain has four times as many triangles. One big terrain is cheaper than many small ones.
:::
