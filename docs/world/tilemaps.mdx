# Tilemaps

<Art name="island" size={110} />

A **tilemap** is a whole level made of square **tiles** painted on a grid: ground, walls, platforms, water, flowers. It is one object and one mesh, however many tiles it has, so big levels stay fast. Tiles you mark **solid** block the player and other physics bodies, and you can change tiles while the game runs (open a door, break a block).

It is made for a 2D side view (see [2D games](./2d-games)), but a tilemap is just a flat grid standing in the 3D world: you can lay it flat as a floor too.

## The tile sheet

The tiles come from a **tile sheet**: a picture made in the [sprite editor](./sprites) where **every frame is one tile**. Make a sprite the size of one tile (for example 64 × 64), draw one tile per frame, and save. Tile **0** is the first frame, tile 1 the second, and so on.

</Row>

### Cut a sheet from an image

Tile art usually comes as one picture full of equal squares. Instead of redrawing it, cut it: **Files → ＋ New → Sprite sheet from an image…** (or, in the Tilemap card, **✂️ New tile sheet → Cut it from an image…**).

1. Pick the picture.
2. Type the size of **one piece** in pixels, such as `64` or `64 x 32`.
3. Give the sheet a name.
4. Tap the pieces to keep (or drag over several). Empty pieces start turned off. **All** and **None** help. Tap **Make sheet**.

<Row>
  <Phone name="cut-pieces" caption="Tap the pieces you want: the blue ones are kept" />
</Row>

The kept pieces go side by side in a new sprite sheet, in reading order (left to right, top to bottom). A sheet can be 4096 pixels wide, so at 64 px that is up to 64 pieces; when the picture has more, it starts with none selected so you can choose. The same works for a character picture: each piece becomes a frame of a [sprite](./sprites).

You can also use the sheet of the **2D Platformer** template (grass and earth pieces, floating platforms, a bush and a mushroom).

## Add a tilemap

**Objects → ＋ Add**, under **Sprites**: **Tilemap**. It starts with the first sprite sheet of your project (change it with **Tile sheet**; **🖌️ New tile sheet** draws one, **✏ Edit** reopens the sheet you picked). A Collider is added too, so solid tiles block.

## Paint

Tap **2D** in the 3D view's toolbar first: it gives a flat front view with a vertical grid, so each cell is easy to hit (see [the 2D view](../editor/viewport#the-2d-view)). Then select the tilemap and tap **🖌️ Paint tiles**. Now a drag in the 3D view paints; a faint grid and a yellow square show the cell under your finger. While painting, one finger paints; use two fingers (or the right mouse button) to move and zoom the view. Tap **✋ Stop painting** when you are done, or select something else.

<Row>
  <Phone name="card-tilemap-painting" caption="Painting on: tools and palette" />
</Row>

| Tool | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **🖌️ Draw** | Paints the chosen tile on every cell you drag over. |
| **🧽 Erase** | Empties the cells you drag over. |
| **🪣 Fill** | Fills the area you tap: connected cells of the same tile (or, on empty ground, the empty space inside what you have painted, plus a margin). Limited to 5000 cells. |

The **palette** shows every tile of the sheet; tap one to choose it (the chosen tile has a blue frame). One drag is one **Undo** step.

## Solid tiles

Right under the palette, a button (**Tile 0: 🧱 Solid ⟳**) shows what the **chosen tile** does; it follows the tile you choose in the palette. Tap it to go to the next kind:

| Kind | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **🧱 Solid** | Blocks from every side (ground, walls). This is the default. |
| **⬆ One-way platform** | Blocks only from above: a character jumps up through it and lands on it. It has a yellow line on top of its thumbnail. |
| **◻ Walkthrough** | Nothing happens: flowers, backgrounds. It has a small ring in the corner of its thumbnail. |

A second button, **🪜 Climbable**, marks the tile as a ladder or vine (a small ladder shows on its thumbnail): see [Climbing](#climbing). A ladder is usually also *Walkthrough*.

Solid tiles that touch are merged into as few boxes as possible, so a long floor is one collider: the player never catches on the seam between two tiles. The boxes are as deep as **Collider depth** says (front to back, in meters).

:::note
Solid tiles block only when the object has a **Collider** component (set to *Auto*). Without it the tilemap is only a picture.
:::

## Auto tiles

Painting every edge and corner of a hill by hand is slow. With **auto tiles** you paint the ground and the engine picks the right piece for each cell: a corner at the corners, a flat top along the top, a middle inside. Erase a cell and the pieces around it round themselves off.

<Row>
  <Phone name="card-tilemap-auto-set" caption="Auto tile sets under the palette" />
</Row>

Set one up in the Tilemap card, under **Auto tiles**:

- **＋ Block (9 pieces)** for ground, walls and hills. You tap the tile for each piece in turn: top left, top, top right, left, middle, right, bottom left, bottom, bottom right. The pieces are laid out as they will meet.
- **＋ Platform (3 pieces)** for a one-tile-high floating platform: left end, middle, right end.

<Row>
  <Phone name="autotile-setup" caption="Tap the tile for each piece; tap a piece above to change it" />
</Row>

Tap a piece in the grid to go back to it, and **Done** when all are filled. Give the set a name. Then tap its name in the list to paint with it (the same drag as **Draw**). To change or redo a set, delete it (🗑) and make it again.

<Row>
  <Phone name="autotile-painted" caption="One drag with the Earth set: corners and edges fit by themselves" />
</Row>

How it works: a cell belongs to a set when its tile is one of the set's pieces. Each cell looks at its four neighbours (up, right, down, left) that belong to the same set and picks its piece from that. So you can also **Draw** a piece by hand, **Erase** or **Fill**, and the neighbouring pieces update. A tile that is not a piece of any set is never changed. In events and scripts, *Erase tile* / `removeTile` and `setTile` also update the pieces next to the changed cell, so digging a hole in the ground leaves rounded edges.

## Climbing

A **Platformer** with **Climb** switched on can climb the tiles you marked **Climbable**: press **up** or **down** next to a ladder to grab it, **up / down** to climb, and let go with **left / right** (to step onto a platform at the top) or **Jump**. Stack climbable tiles for the ladder, and put a one-way platform tile beside its top so the hero can step onto it. Objects tagged `ladder` work the same, for a ladder that is one picture.

## Settings

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Tile sheet** | The picture with the tiles. |
| **Tile size (m)** | How big one tile is in the world. 1 = one meter per tile. |
| **Color** / **Opacity** | Tint and fade the whole tilemap. |
| **Sort order** | Higher draws in front of lower, like sprites. Use it to put a scenery tilemap behind the level. |
| **Edges** | **Smooth** blends pixels; **Pixel (hard)** keeps pixel art crisp. |
| **Collider depth (m)** | How thick the collision boxes are. |
| **Clear** | Empty the whole map. |

Cells are counted in tiles from the object's position: cell **(0, 0)** is the square from the position up and to the right (X right, Y up). Negative numbers go left and down.

## Events and scripts

| Block | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Paint tile … at cell (x, y)** | Puts a tile in one cell. The collision follows at once. |
| **Erase the tile at cell (x, y)** | Empties one cell. |

In scripts, on the tilemap object:

```js
entity.setTile(3, 0, 2);      // cell (3, 0) becomes tile 2
entity.getTile(3, 0);         // 2   (-1 when the cell is empty)
entity.removeTile(3, 0);      // empty again
const p = game.find('Player').getWorldPosition();
const c = entity.cellAt(p.x, p.y);   // { x, y }: the cell at a world position
```

A negative tile number erases the cell. Use `cellAt` to find the cell under a hero or a tap: *"if the cell under the hero is a spike tile, hurt it"*.

## Ideas

- **Doors and blocks:** a switch calls `removeTile` on a wall, and the way is open.
- **Digging:** on tap, find the cell with `cellAt` and erase it.
- **Two layers:** add a second tilemap with a lower **Sort order** for scenery that does not block (give it no Collider).
